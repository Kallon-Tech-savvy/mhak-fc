From the Ground Up / Founding note 02 / Identity

An identity in progress

Before MHAK has a playing squad, it has an opportunity to decide what the club will stand for—and to test whether the symbols match the culture it intends to create.

Working founding note. This article records MHAK's current intentions and design decisions. It is not evidence of completed football operations. A public publication date has not been established.

What the identity aims to express

MHAK's brand exploration began with a choice to emphasise belonging and collective commitment. The underlying idea is that individuals can contribute to one recognisable whole without losing their responsibilities to each other.

For a young club, those qualities may be better communicated through repeated behaviour than a long slogan. The crest and standalone symbol should help people recognise the club, while the club's conduct gives them meaning over time.

The symbol decision

A connected geometric symbol was selected after exploring forms associated with unity and shared identity. A later refinement of its terminal ends was proposed, but replacing the selected master remains subject to approval and production checks.

The artwork should remain practical at small digital sizes and in one-colour printing. Distinctiveness, audience interpretation and embroidery performance still require evidence; a design explanation cannot prove those qualities on its own.

The visual language

The proposed digital system uses Obsidian Blue, Dark Orange, Steel Grey, Black and White. The intention is to balance discipline and institutional confidence with the energy of football culture. This palette is a working design choice, not evidence that these colours resonate with prospective players.

What an identity must earn

A good club mark is memorable. A credible club identity also requires commitments that hold up under pressure: clear expectations, consistent treatment and honest communication when plans change.

As MHAK builds coaching, team and community activity, real experiences—not graphics alone—should decide whether the promise of belonging is becoming true.

What happens next

  • Keep the standalone mark and crest visually coherent.
  • Test recognition and one-colour reproduction before production purchases.
  • Learn what intended players actually understand from the identity without an explanation.
  • Continue telling the club's actual founding decisions rather than depicting operations that do not exist.
