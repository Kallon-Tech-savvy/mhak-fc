From the Ground Up / Founding note 01 / Purpose

Why MHAK FC?

A football club can be more than a group assembled for the next match. MHAK's ambition is to build a shared identity and an environment where improvement is taken seriously.

Working founding note. This article records MHAK's current intentions and design decisions. It is not evidence of completed football operations. A public publication date has not been established.

The starting belief

MHAK FC is being developed with a straightforward ambition: establish a football institution in Sierra Leone that is built deliberately, not only around short-term results. The founding direction puts identity, belonging, discipline and collective commitment at the centre.

These are principles we have chosen to pursue. They are not yet achievements or proof of a successful player-development programme.

What belonging should require

In the proposed club culture, players would represent something larger than themselves. Shared responsibility should also apply to coaches and leadership. Discipline must not become a rule for players alone while decision-makers remain exempt.

That standard is useful only when it becomes observable: reliable communication, consistent coaching expectations, constructive feedback and fair treatment. The systems for delivering those experiences have not yet been established.

The development promise—and its limits

The working promise is “an environment to improve.” It describes a direction, not a guarantee of player performance, professional contracts or selection opportunities. A suitable volunteer coach remains to be confirmed, and regular training methods have not been defined.

Until those conditions exist, the club should ask people to understand its intentions rather than believe it already operates a proven development pathway.

What happens next

  • Clarify standards and responsibilities that leadership itself can uphold.
  • Explore a feasible, consistent coaching arrangement.
  • Develop a realistic training structure before inviting participants into a programme.
  • Document actual milestones and adjust the public story when evidence changes.

That is the kind of progress this journal should capture: decisions that turn a founding idea into a real club, one confirmed step at a time.
